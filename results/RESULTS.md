Survival model selected for the profit analysis: **Cox (elastic net, strat. contract)** - chosen by 3-fold CV C-index on the training data only (CV C-index: Cox 0.863, Weibull AFT 0.862, forest 0.849).

**Read this table carefully.**

- Against the like-for-like classifiers (same features, no tenure), the survival models are clearly better at ranking customers for the 12-month horizon (AUC@12m 0.914 vs 0.857 for XGBoost) and **much** better calibrated (mean absolute decile calibration error 0.024 vs 0.100; the classifiers predict *ever*-churn probability, not 12-month probability).
- The classifiers **with tenure as a feature** score higher still (XGBoost + tenure AUC@12m 0.936). This is not evidence that they are better: ranking customers by short tenure alone gives AUC@12m = 1.000, because tenure is the duration that *defines* the outcome (every customer who churned within 12 months has a shorter tenure than every customer who did not). Under the paper's formulation - risk measured from sign-up - tenure is not available at decision time, so these rows are a leakage reference, not a fair baseline. They are reported because the usual Telco churn exercise includes tenure and the comparison would otherwise be hidden.

## 6. What these results do and do not show

- **No intervention data.** q and c are assumptions; profits are an *assumed-effect* evaluation, not a measured uplift.
- **The refined rule is aligned with the evaluation by construction** (same definition of margin lost inside the horizon). The independent comparison is ranking quality: survival risk vs classifier probability, scored on the same outcome.
- **Risk is measured from sign-up**, as in the paper (S(t|x) with t = tenure). Applying it to existing customers properly needs conditional survival, S(t0+H)/S(t0), and a landmark-style evaluation; the dataset has no snapshot date to support that evaluation.
- **Service flags are end-of-observation values**, possibly the plan held when a customer left; read effects as associations.
- **Single public teaching dataset**, one split family; the bootstrap and 5-split checks show the gap between survival risk and the like-for-like classifiers is modest relative to noise, while the calibration and time-dependent-AUC gaps are large.
- **Proportional hazards:** unstratified Cox violates PH for the one-year contract dummy (p = 0.0002); stratifying on contract fixes that, but MultipleLines, n_addons and (borderline) Partner still show evidence of non-proportional effects (`results/proportional_hazards_test.csv`). The Weibull AFT and forest are alternatives that relax this.
